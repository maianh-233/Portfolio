Somewhere beneath the same sky, a thousand wishes begin to rise.

Every magical night begins somewhere.

Before the sky begins to glow, Chiang Mai welcomes you with quiet streets, ancient temples, warm lights and the gentle rhythm of northern Thailand.

Three things to do before the lanterns rise.

  1. i.

    Wander through Old Chiang Mai

    Walk inside the old moat walls at golden hour, past teak houses, small coffee bars and monks crossing temple courtyards.

    Golden hour, inside the moat

  2. ii.

    Discover Lanna culture

    Learn why the north lights lanterns at all: the Lanna calendar, the full moon of the twelfth month, and the wish to let go of the year behind you.

    With a local guide

  3. iii.

    Follow the golden lights

    As evening falls, paper lanterns are hung over gates and lanes. You will stroll beneath them before the festival night begins.

    Tha Phae Gate, after dark

Three days, five moments

The journey we dream about.

  1. Day 1, afternoon

    01. A Quiet Afternoon

    Settle into Chiang Mai slowly. Taste khao soi at a family kitchen, wander the lanes and feel the easy pace of the city.

    About 4 hours

  2. Day 1, evening

    02. A City of Lanterns

    See traditional Lanna lanterns glowing over temple gates, explore the festival grounds and hear the stories behind them from a local guide.

    About 3 hours

  3. Day 2, dusk

    03. The Moment of Light

    Join the lantern evening at a licensed event venue. Timing and access follow the official schedule and conditions on the day.

    Evening, schedule confirmed each year

  4. Day 2, night

    04. A Thousand Wishes

    The moment the lanterns rise together and the whole sky turns gold. Most guests tell us this is the minute they remember first.

    A few unforgettable minutes

  5. Day 3, morning

    05. A Morning to Remember

    Wake to a calm city. Visit a hilltop temple or a morning market, keep a few souvenirs, then continue your journey.

    About 5 hours

Some places are beautiful. Others make you feel something.

Wat

Temples and Lanna architecture

Carved teak gables, naga stairways and the quiet ruins of Wat Chedi Luang, best seen early before the heat.

Khantoke

Northern Thai food

Khao soi, sai oua sausage and sticky rice eaten with your hands at a khantoke dinner.

Night markets

The Sunday Walking Street on Ratchadamnoen Road: handmade crafts, street snacks and live folk music.

Lantern-lit streets

During festival week, Tha Phae Gate and the lanes around it glow with hanging lanterns.

Krathong on the water

When Loy Krathong is held, small floating lanterns of banana leaf and flowers drift along the Ping River.
