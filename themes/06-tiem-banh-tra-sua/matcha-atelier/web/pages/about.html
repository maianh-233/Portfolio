Find us

Slow, on purpose

A small tea house in Saigon, learning from the Japanese way of tea and a slower pace of life.

Inspired by chado, the way of tea

The way of tea teaches that preparing tea matters as much as drinking it. The host readies the room, chooses the bowl and wipes each tool in front of the guest. We keep that spirit, not the ceremony.

Our bar is open so you can see every step. No loud music, no screens. Only the sound of the bamboo whisk and the smell of freshly sifted tea.

Ichigo ichie: one time, one meeting.

A saying from the Japanese tea ceremony

How we choose our leaves

Origin
Leaves from one family farm in Shirakawa, Uji. We know the grower and the harvest week.
Shade
Plants rest under straw shade for about three weeks, which brings sweetness and umami.
Freshness
Stone-ground, packed in small tins and finished within 30 days of opening.

A balance of three tastes

Umami

It comes from shaded leaves. Tea leads here, so our cakes always step back.

Sweetness

From red bean, palm sugar and seasonal fruit, about a third less than a standard recipe.

Bitterness

A little bitterness at the finish wakes the palate. We keep it rather than hide it.

Slow living, in small rituals

We have no fast wifi and no time limit on your table. Drag to look around.

Stay as long as you like

There is no time limit on any table. A cup deserves to be finished.

Ceramics with fingerprints

Hand-thrown bowls are never perfect, and that is what we love about them.

A menu that follows the seasons

Our wagashi change weekly: cherry blossom in spring, chestnut in autumn.

A quiet afternoon

From 2 pm to 4 pm the music goes off. Only the whisk and soft conversation remain.

Your table is waiting
