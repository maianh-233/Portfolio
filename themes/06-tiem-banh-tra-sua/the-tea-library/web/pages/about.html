A Place to Pause

The story of a room full of books, slow pots of tea and the people who like to stay a while.

Our story

It began with one bookshelf and one teapot

Written by our founder, Saigon, autumn

THE TEA LIBRARY grew out of a very small habit. Every Sunday afternoon we brewed a pot of tea, picked an old book and read until the light ran out. Friends began to drop by with books of their own. The shelf grew longer, the teapot grew larger, and the room slowly became a place.

We took our cues from the old libraries of Europe and from tea rooms where people sit for hours and nobody hurries them. Oak, warm lamps, yellowed paper and the sound of a spoon against porcelain. Everything here is chosen to help you forget the clock.

“We do not sell hurry. We serve time.”

Today the room holds more than 1,400 books, twenty-four teas and an oven that starts work at five each morning. The spirit is the same as on the first Sunday: a good cup of tea, a pastry still warm from the oven and a page you have not read yet.

Chapter one

Tea culture

From Shan Tuyet grown on the mountains of Ha Giang to afternoon tea in London, every culture has its own way of stopping. We keep those rituals: a porcelain pot, water at the right degree and time to wait.

Chapter two

Tea and pastry

Each pastry is made to sit beside a particular tea: butter scones with Earl Grey, madeleines with Darjeeling, lemon cake with green tea. Ask us for the pairing of the day.

Chapter three, the room

Window light, the smell of old wood and a seat nobody will ask you to leave.

Thirty-two seats, a quiet reading corner upstairs and a long table by the west window.
