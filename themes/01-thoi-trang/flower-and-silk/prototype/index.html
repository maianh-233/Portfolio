It began with my grandmother's silk slip.

She kept it folded in tissue paper for fifty years. When I finally held it, the lace was still perfect, and I wondered why the things we wear closest to us are so often the least loved.

Lilou is my answer: pieces made with the same patience, for women who deserve to feel cherished on a Tuesday.

Linh Tran, founder

Style notes, 6 min read

How to build a vanity corner that feels like a love letter

Start with light. A small table near a window gives you soft, honest light that makes getting ready feel calm instead of rushed.

Add one thing that lives and changes: a few garden roses in a glass jar. Replace them on Sundays and the corner never feels static.

Keep only what you love on the surface. Brushes in a lace-cut holder, one perfume, one mirror with a pretty frame. The rest goes in a drawer.

Rituals, 4 min read

The slow Sunday morning, a small manifesto

No alarm. Phone in the other room. A book you started three weeks ago and a coffee you actually drink while it is warm.

Wear something that feels good against your skin, even if nobody will see it. That is the whole point.

Our team keeps one rule: one slow morning a week is not a luxury. It is maintenance for the heart.

Care guide, 5 min read

Caring for silk and lace so they last a lifetime

Hand wash in cool water with a few drops of silk wash. Never wring; press the water out between two towels instead.

Dry flat and away from direct sun, which fades blush tones faster than you think.

Store bras with cups nested, not folded in half, and keep a little lavender sachet in the drawer.
