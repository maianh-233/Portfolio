Designed with the desert, not against it.

SANDLINE began with a question: how little can a building add to a landscape and still offer shelter, comfort and calm?

The design idea

The architects spent a full year on the ridge before drawing a single wall. They mapped where the wind drops, where shade falls at noon and which hollows stay cool after dark.

The plan grew from those notes. Residences step down the slope like strata of rock, each one turned a few degrees to catch the morning sun and avoid the western glare.

“We wanted guests to feel the weight of the walls and the lightness of the sky at the same time.”

Inés Calvo Ruiz, lead architect

Inside, the palette is reduced to what the site already offered: sand-coloured concrete, local stone, oiled oak and wide panes of low-iron glass.

Four materials, nothing more.

01

Stone

Sandstone quarried forty kilometres away, laid in thick dry walls that hold the night's coolness well into the day.

02

Concrete

Mixed with local sand so its colour matches the ground. Board-formed, left raw, never painted.

03

Wood

Oiled oak for everything the hand touches: doors, benches, window seats and the long dining tables.

04

Glass

Deep-set panes shaded by the roof above, so the view stays open while the heat stays out.

Five rules we built by.

Rule one

Build low.

No roof rises above the ridge line. From the valley road, SANDLINE is almost invisible.

Follow the sun.

Every opening is placed by the sun path: soft east light in, hard west light out.

Use the ground.

Earth from the foundations became the walls.

Keep water still.

Pools are mirrors, not fountains.

Leave space empty.

Courtyards, gaps and long walls with nothing on them give the eye somewhere to rest.

Studies in light and mass.

The land was here first. We simply made room to stay.
